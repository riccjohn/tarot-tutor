There is no correct way to shuffle. What matters is that the deck ends up in an order you did not choose, and that the method is one you can repeat without thinking about it. Below is a plain method, with the common variations and the small decisions that come up.

## Before you shuffle

Settle on a question, or decide to go without one. Both work. A question gives the card something to answer, and an open draw lets the card set the topic. If you use a question, make it one a card can actually respond to. "What should I pay attention to in this conversation?" gives you something to look at. "Will I get the job?" asks the image to do something it was not built for.

A flat surface helps, mostly because it stops you fussing with cards that slide around.

## Shuffling

Three methods cover almost everyone.

- **Overhand.** Hold the deck in one hand and pull small packets off the top into the other. It is gentle on the cards, and it is the easiest to do at a desk.
- **Riffle.** Split the deck in two and let the corners interleave. It mixes thoroughly, but it is harder on the cards and takes practice.
- **Table mix.** Spread the cards face down and swirl them around, then gather them back up. It is slow, and it is very good at randomizing.

Shuffle until the deck feels unfamiliar to you. Ten to twenty seconds is usually enough. Stopping is a judgment call, and there is no signal you are supposed to wait for.

## Cutting and drawing

Cut the deck into two or three piles and restack them in any order, then draw from the top. Some readers skip the cut, and some draw from the middle of a spread-out fan. All of these are fine, as long as you are not looking at the faces while you choose.

Draw one card, turn it over, and put it down where you can see it fully. If you are using reversals, note which way it landed before you touch it. The reversals lesson covers what to do with that.

## When something odd happens

A card jumps out of the deck while you shuffle. Some readers treat it as the draw, and others put it back, on the grounds that it did not come from a fair mix. Decide which rule you follow before it happens, so you are not deciding in the middle of a reading.

You draw a card you do not like. You can read it as it is, and that is usually the more interesting choice. You can also draw again, and treat the second as a separate reading. What you should avoid is drawing until you get something comfortable, because then the deck is only agreeing with you.

You draw a card you already know well. Look at it as though it were new. Describe what is actually on it before you reach for what you remember it means.

## Afterward

Read the card in the order the next lesson describes. When you are done, put the card back in the deck and shuffle again if you plan to draw more.
