A tarot card's structure — its suit, its number, its symbols — was composed on purpose. Reading it is closer to reading a deliberately built visual argument than to predicting the future. That is the whole mechanism, and this is the one place the site says it outright. What you do with that argument is yours to decide.

## What you are looking at

Every card in the deck carries three layers of design, and they stack.

The suit tells you the territory. Swords concerns thought and conflict, Cups concerns feeling, Wands concerns drive, and Pentacles concerns the material world. The number tells you the stage: an Ace is a beginning, a Three is the first real complication, a Ten is a cycle at its limit. The symbols are what the artist actually put on the card, such as a heart, a sky, a figure with a posture.

Those three things are fixed. They are what the card is, and they can be described the way you would describe a painting. Nothing about them depends on your mood or on what you hope to hear.

## What a reading adds

Once you have described the structure, you can ask what it is conventionally taken to mean. This is a second, softer layer, and it is worth keeping separate from the first. A common reading is a lens that many readers have found useful, offered as one lens among several. It is not the card's hidden verdict.

The separation matters in practice. If you can say "these are the three swords, this is the heart, this is the rain" before you say "so this is about heartbreak," you will always know which parts of your interpretation come from the card and which come from you and the tradition you borrowed from. Both are legitimate. Confusing them is where readings go vague.

## What a reading isn't

A reading is not a forecast, and it does not need to be one to be worth doing. It is a structured prompt for reflection: a fixed image, with fixed features, that you meet on a particular day with a particular question in your head. The card does not change. You do, and the interesting part of a reading usually happens in the gap between the two.

It is also not a test with a correct answer. Traditions disagree about a lot, including the elemental attribution of whole suits, and this site says so where it matters. Two careful readers can look at the same card and land in different places without either being wrong.

## A place to start

Draw one card, or open one from the card index. Before you read anything about it, spend a minute describing it to yourself in plain terms. Then read what the site says and notice where you agree, and where you would put it differently.
