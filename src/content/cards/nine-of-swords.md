name: Nine of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A figure in a white nightshirt sitting upright at the edge of a
          bed, both hands pressed over the face
      note: The posture is one of wakefulness and distress, held privately.
          The figure is alone in the room, so whatever is being turned over
          is being turned over in the mind.
    - element: Nine swords hung horizontally in a stack on a black wall above
          the figure, their hilts at the left and their blades running out
          to the right edge
      note: The swords are lined up rather than pointed at anyone. They
          hover behind the figure and above the head, which is where thought
          sits, and they do not touch the body.
    - element: A solid black background behind the swords and the figure, with
          the numeral IX in white at the top
      note: The darkness is total and flat. It reads as night, and as the
          way a worry can fill the whole frame while the room itself is
          small.
    - element: A quilt across the foot of the bed, patterned in squares of
          red roses and pale blue and yellow marks
      note: The quilt is the most cheerful and detailed thing on the card,
          and it is in the foreground, in reach. Often read as comfort that
          is present but unnoticed while the mind is loud.
    - element: A carved wooden bed frame in orange and brown, with a small
          panel of a carved scene along its side
      note: The panel shows a scene set apart from the sleeper, a story
          carved in wood. It reads as a reminder that the moment is one
          panel within a larger picture, not the whole of it.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a night of worry, when
    thought is at its loudest and the cause is often largest in the mind.
    The swords hang above the figure and do not touch them, which is often
    taken to mean that the pain here is anticipation, replay, and dread
    more than an event. The Nine is the number of taking stock near the end
    of a stretch, and in Swords the stock-taking arrives as a rush of
    thoughts. Readers often pair the card with the way problems look
    different in daylight, or once they are said aloud to someone.
invitationPrompt: What is the loudest thought in your head today, and what
    would you say to it if it were a friend telling you the same thing?
