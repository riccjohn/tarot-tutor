name: Wheel of Fortune
symbols:
    - element: A large orange wheel with eight spokes, hanging in a blue sky among clouds, with the letters T, A, R, O around its rim, along with smaller marks between them
      note: The wheel fills the centre of the card and turns in open air with nothing supporting it. Conventionally read as cycles that move on their own schedule, with the lettered rim often taken as a play on the deck's own name.
    - element: A blue sphinx in a striped headcloth perched on top of the wheel, holding a sword upright
      note: The sphinx sits at the highest point and is still while the wheel turns. Often read as balance or judgement held at the top of the cycle, though where the sword points is a matter for the reader.
    - element: A red figure with the head of a jackal rising along the right side of the wheel, and a yellow snake curving down the left side
      note: One figure is climbing up one side, and the other is sliding down the other. Conventionally read as the two directions of any turning, with ascent and descent both part of one motion.
    - element: A winged man, an eagle, a bull, and a lion, one in each corner of the card, all with wings
      note: The four sit among the clouds outside the wheel, and each has a book open before it. Often read as four fixed points that stay put while the wheel turns, and read against the four suits or the four seasons.
    - element: A pale blue, cloudy sky with no ground or horizon visible
      note: Nothing in the scene shows where the wheel stands. Read as the cycle being a larger frame than any single place, which fits a card about what moves on its own.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading takes this card as cycles and turning points, the parts of a life that move on their own schedule. The wheel keeps turning, one figure rises while another falls, and the sphinx at the top stays steady. Coming straight after the Hermit's solitude, it marks the wider world's motion returning. On this reading the card is less about luck than about noticing which parts of a situation are turning and which are steady.
invitationPrompt: Where in your life is something turning, and where are you the one holding still?
