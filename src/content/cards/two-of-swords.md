---
name: Two of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A seated figure in a long white robe, blindfolded with a white
          band across the eyes
      note: Conventionally read as a decision made without looking, or as a
          deliberate refusal to look until the inputs are in. The blindfold
          is worn, not forced on, so it can be either.
    - element: Two straight swords held upright, crossed at the wrists over the
          chest, their blades angling out to form a V above the head
      note: The two blades are equal in size and angle. Neither is raised
          higher or held more firmly, which is the number's balance shown as
          a posture.
    - element: A calm expanse of pale blue water behind the figure, with
          jagged rocks rising at the left and right edges and a distant
          shoreline
      note: The water is flat and the rocks sit outside the figure's reach.
          Read as feeling that is held at a distance while the mind works,
          or as obstacles that can be seen but not yet approached.
    - element: A thin crescent moon in the upper right of the sky, and the
          numeral II at the top centre
      note: The moon is often read as partial light, enough to see by but not
          to see everything. The numeral confirms that this is the pairing
          card, not the sequence's end.
    - element: A plain stone bench under the figure, set on a flat grey
          paving
      note: The figure sits still on solid ground. Nothing on the card is
          moving, so the tension comes from the crossed blades and the
          blindfold alone.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a stalemate of reasoning
    — two arguments or two options weighted equally, with no verdict yet.
    The crossed swords and the blindfold together read as guarding one's own
    position while refusing to look at the choice directly. Some readers
    see that as avoidance; others see it as a pause taken until the facts
    are in. Either way the balance holds only as long as neither side is
    chosen, and the card puts the choosing back on the person sitting in
    it.
invitationPrompt: Which two things are you holding at equal weight right now,
    and what would you need to know before you took your hands away from one
    of them?
