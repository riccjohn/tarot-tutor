---
name: Seven of Pentacles
symbols:
    - element: A figure in an orange tunic and blue sleeves, leaning on a long
          wooden-handled tool with both hands crossed on top and his chin
          resting on them
      note: The posture is that of someone who has stopped work in order to
          look. His tool is planted in the ground, so the labor is paused,
          not abandoned.
    - element: A dense green vine or bush heaped up at the left, with six
          pentacles growing among its leaves
      note: The coins hang on the plant like fruit, which makes the suit's
          emblem something that grows and ripens rather than something
          minted. Six have come in, so the growth has visibly paid off.
    - element: A seventh pentacle lying on the ground at the figure's feet,
          apart from the vine
      note: It is the only coin not on the plant, close to his boot, and it
          is often read as the harvested one or the next decision. The
          card does not say whether it has been picked or has fallen.
    - element: The figure's gaze, directed down and to the left at the coins
      note: He looks at the work without touching it. This is the card's
          central action, an assessment of whether what was planted is worth
          the effort still to come.
    - element: A bare grey sky over a strip of purple and blue ground at the
          horizon
      note: The setting is empty of other people, so this is one person's
          judgment of one person's work.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a pause to look over
    the work and ask whether it is worth continuing. It fits the Seven's
    role as a test, and in Pentacles the test is patience. The vine has borne
    fruit, and the figure is deciding whether to keep tending it, wait for
    more, or move on. The reading stays with the evaluation, not the verdict,
    since he has not yet picked up the tool or the coin. Readers often bring
    it to questions about return on effort, and the answer stays with the
    person leaning on the tool.
invitationPrompt: What have you been tending for a long time, and how would
    you know it was worth carrying on?
