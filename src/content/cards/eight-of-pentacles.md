name: Eight of Pentacles
symbols:
    - element: A young craftsman in a blue tunic and red hose, seated on a
          wooden bench, holding a mallet in one hand and a chisel in the
          other
      note: The figure is absorbed in the work, head bent and eyes on the
          tool. Nothing on the card competes with the task, so attention
          itself is what is shown.
    - element: A coin lying on a wooden block in front of him, with the
          chisel set against a pentacle he is cutting into its face
      note: The emblem of the suit is being made here, by hand, one at a
          time. The star is not given to the figure; it is cut.
    - element: Six finished pentacles hung in a column on a tall wooden post
          at the right, and a seventh lying on the ground at the left
      note: With the coin on the block, that makes eight, in three states
          (displayed, set down, and in progress). The pile of finished work is
          evidence that the repetition has been going on for some time.
    - element: A small town with a tower and red roofs in the far left
          distance, beyond the bench
      note: The workshop sits apart from the town, on open ground. The
          craftsman is at a distance from the market his coins are for.
    - element: A large empty grey field filling the upper left of the card
      note: The composition leaves most of the card blank around one small
          bent figure, so the effort looks large against the space, and the
          work goes on without an audience.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as craft, skill built
    through repetition. The Eight is a number of committed movement, and in
    Pentacles that movement is patient practice, each coin cut as carefully as
    the one before. The row of finished coins shows what steady work
    accumulates. It reads as an encouraging card for anyone learning a trade
    or improving at something by doing it again. It also has a narrower side,
    since the figure sits alone, at one bench, doing one thing, and readers
    sometimes take that as a question about when the practice serves the
    person and when it has become the whole day.
invitationPrompt: What are you getting better at by doing it over and over,
    and what does the repetition give you?
