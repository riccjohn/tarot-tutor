name: Page of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A young figure in a maroon tunic, yellow sleeves and leggings,
          and red boots, standing on a grassy hilltop and looking back over
          the shoulder
      note: The Page is drawn young and lightly balanced, weight on one
          foot. The backward glance keeps the figure alert to what is
          behind while still facing forward, which is the rank's attention
          first, action later.
    - element: A long straight sword held upright in both hands across the
          chest, its point angled up and out of the frame
      note: Both hands are on the hilt, gripping it firmly, but the blade is
          not aimed at anything. The emblem is being held and examined, not
          yet used.
    - element: Wind-tossed red-brown hair, and a small flock of birds flying
          in the upper left of the sky
      note: The hair and the birds both show movement in the air. Air is the
          element this site pairs with Swords, and here it is visible
          working on the figure.
    - element: Large rounded grey clouds packed behind the figure and around
          the hill, against a bright blue sky
      note: The clouds fill the middle of the card and crowd the figure's
          sides. They read as a mind full of activity, thoughts piled up
          around one point of attention.
    - element: A windblown dark tree at the far left edge and a strip of blue
          water and distant mountains at the right
      note: The landscape is open in both directions. The tree leans with the
          wind, so the whole scene, not only the figure, is in motion.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats the Page of Swords as a learner of
    thought and speech — someone curious, quick, and eager to say what they
    see before they have fully worked out how to say it well. The Page is
    the beginning of the court, so the sharpness of the suit has arrived and
    is still being tried out. As a person, this can be a newcomer with
    direct questions; as a stage, it is enthusiasm that runs a little ahead
    of skill, and the gap is allowed to be there. The alertness in the
    posture is the strength of the card, and the unaimed blade is its
    reminder that the tool is still new in the hand.
