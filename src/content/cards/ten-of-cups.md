name: Ten of Cups
symbols:
    - element: Ten golden cups set in a curved arc across the sky, like the
          span of a rainbow
      note: The ten cups are the whole suit at full count, gathered into a
          single shape overhead. They do not sit in anyone's hands; they hang
          above the scene as a sign of what the people below are looking at.
    - element: A band of pink, yellow, and green bristling rays around the arc
          of cups
      note: The colored band makes the arc read as a rainbow, the conventional
          image of a promise kept after weather has cleared. It curves over
          the entire top of the card, framing everything beneath it.
    - element: Two adults seen from behind with their inner arms around each
          other, each raising the other arm toward the cups
      note: Their raised arms point at the arc and their bodies lean together
          into a single shape. The gesture is open and shared, and readers
          take it as gratitude or welcome more than as a claim of ownership.
    - element: Two small children dancing hand in hand to the right of the
          adults
      note: The children face each other and move freely, playing rather than
          looking up. They show that the celebration is part of ordinary
          family life, with the adults' gesture and the children's play
          happening side by side.
    - element: A red-roofed house among trees on a green rise, with a winding
          river and rolling hills beyond
      note: This is the setting the whole family stands in, a home in a
          settled landscape. The house is small and ordinary and set in
          the middle distance, so the happiness is tied to a lived place
          rather than a grand one.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as emotional fulfillment, a
    shared and settled happiness. The four figures and the house make it the
    suit's picture of home and belonging, and readers apply it to family,
    close friendship, or any community where people feel at ease with each
    other. The Ten is a completion, and here it arrives as fulfillment and not
    overload. Some readers hear it as the natural resting point of everything
    the earlier Cups cards were reaching for, and others see it as a picture
    of what enough looks like.
invitationPrompt: Where do you already feel at home, and who is standing
    there with you?
