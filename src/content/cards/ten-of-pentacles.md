name: Ten of Pentacles
symbols:
    - element: An elderly man with white hair, seated at the left in a heavy
          robe patterned with crest-like emblems, looking toward the others
      note: He is the oldest figure in the suit and the only one sitting at
          rest. The pattern on his robe reads as a family or house emblem,
          which ties the wealth on this card to lineage as much as to the
          person wearing it.
    - element: A man in a blue cloak, seen from behind, and a woman in a
          red dress beside him, with a small child at the right edge
      note: Three generations are in the frame at once. The man's back is
          to the viewer, the woman looks off to the left, and the child
          stands close beside her dress. The security shown is shared across
          a household, not held by one person.
    - element: Two white dogs at the couple's feet, both facing left toward
          the group
      note: The dogs are conventionally read as loyalty and ease. They sit
          close to the household without any sign of alarm, which adds to
          the settled feeling of the scene.
    - element: A stone archway across the top of the card, with a castle
          tower, red-roofed buildings, and a tall brick tower behind the
          figures
      note: The setting is built and long established, and the figures stand
          under an arch of their own history. Nothing here is being started,
          and the structure has been carried across time.
    - element: Ten pentacles scattered across the whole card, overlapping the
          arch, the figures, and the ground alike
      note: The full ten is the suit completely expressed. The coins are not
          held by anyone, so the wealth reads as part of the place and the
          people together.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as lasting security,
    resources shared across a family or community. The Ten completes the
    suit's cycle, and in Pentacles that completion looks like something built
    that will outlast the person who built it. The old man, the couple, the
    child, and the dogs make a household in which the effort of earlier cards
    has settled into something others can live inside. It is often read as
    inheritance in a broad sense, covering money, home, values, or a craft
    handed down. Some readers stress the weight of it as well, since a Ten
    can arrive as fulfillment or as obligation.
invitationPrompt: What have you received from those before you that you are
    now responsible for keeping or passing on?
