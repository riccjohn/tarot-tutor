---
name: Five of Cups
symbols:
    - element: A figure in a long black cloak, seen from behind with the head
          bowed, standing in the middle of the card
      note: The cloak covers the whole body and the face is turned away from
          us, so the feeling is carried by the posture alone. The figure faces
          the spilled cups, not the viewer.
    - element: Three golden cups lying on their sides at the lower left, with
          red and green liquid running out of them onto the ground
      note: These are the cups the figure is attending to. The spill is
          finished and can't be undone, which is the card's plain image of
          disappointment, a loss that has already happened.
    - element: Two upright golden cups standing at the lower right, behind
          the figure's shoulder
      note: The two cups are full and standing but sit behind the figure,
          outside its line of sight. Their position is the card's central
          question, whether attention is on what has spilled or on what
          remains.
    - element: A river running across the middle ground from left to right,
          with a stone bridge and a hill on the far right
      note: Water is the suit's element, and here it is moving away across the
          scene. The bridge is a crossing point to the figure's other side,
          and it is often read as a way forward that is not yet in view.
    - element: A small castle on a hill at the far left, above the spilled
          cups
      note: A distant dwelling or refuge, small and out of the way. It is
          usually read as home or safety that remains within reach.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as disappointment, and
    specifically the way attention holds on what has spilled rather than what
    remains. The figure grieves the three cups on the ground while two stand
    upright behind, and the river and bridge beyond point to a way forward that
    is not yet being looked at. The feeling is read as real and worth
    acknowledging rather than hurrying past. Whether and when attention
    shifts is left to the reader.
invitationPrompt: What are you holding your attention on right now, and what
    else is standing just outside your view?
