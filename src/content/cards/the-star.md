name: The Star
symbols:
    - element: One large yellow eight-pointed star at the top centre, ringed
          by seven smaller white eight-pointed stars
      note: The large star is the card's only fixed point of reference in
          an otherwise open sky. The counts, one and seven, are
          conventionally read as a central light and the many that
          surround it.
    - element: A kneeling, unclothed figure with loose blonde hair, one knee
          on the ground and one foot resting on the surface of a pool
      note: With nothing worn or carried but the two jugs, the figure is
          drawn without protection or display. The pose is bent and
          unhurried, which sets the mood of the card as tending, not
          striving.
    - element: Two jugs, one emptying into the pool on the left and the other
          pouring onto the ground on the right
      note: One stream returns to the water and one soaks into the land.
          Conventionally read as giving in two directions at once, to
          what is felt and to what is grown.
    - element: A small round pool with rings spreading across its surface,
          and a thin trickle of water running off from the land into channels
      note: The rings show the water disturbed only by the pour, so the
          surface is otherwise still. The runoff suggests the water
          continues past the frame.
    - element: A leafy tree on the right with a red-and-orange bird perched
          on it, above green ground dotted with small flowers
      note: The bird and the flowers are the only signs of other living
          things. Set against the sparse foreground, they suggest the
          landscape recovering as opposed to already full.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as renewed hope, an
    unhurried and steady openness after upheaval. It is often read as
    restoration that does not return things to how they were, since the
    figure pours out what she has and the ground receives it. In the
    sequence it is the first quiet card after the Tower, and the calm sky
    and open landscape carry that change of pace.
invitationPrompt: What would it look like to give something today without
    needing to see what it becomes?
