name: Queen of Cups
symbols:
    - element: A crowned woman seated in profile on a stone throne, facing
          left, in a white gown with a rippling wave-like pattern
      note: The gown ripples like water and merges into the stream at her
          feet, so she appears to be part of the element she governs. The
          seated pose is the Queen's mark, someone who has arrived and does
          not need to move.
    - element: A large, ornate golden cup with a closed lid, a small cross on
          top, and winged figures for handles, held in both hands
      note: Most cups in the suit are open, and this one is closed. It
          is commonly read as feeling that is deep and kept, held with care
          rather than poured out. She looks steadily at it, and the cup
          shares more of her attention than the viewer does.
    - element: A throne carved with shell forms across the back and small
          cherubs along the top of the arch
      note: Shells and cherubs bring in the sea and the sense of a gentle,
          protective guardianship. A small carved figure at the base of the
          throne belongs to the same watery imagery.
    - element: The sea washing against the edge of her seat, with water
          running around her feet
      note: The Queen sits right at the shore with the tide reaching her, in
          contact with the element without being taken by it. The water is
          the suit's own, and here she remains composed while it moves around
          her.
    - element: A shore scattered with pebbles and shells in yellow, green,
          and orange, and a pale blue sky
      note: The ground under the throne is made of small, collected things,
          and the sky is clear. The scene is calm and quietly detailed,
          which matches the Queen's inward, perceptive character.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats the Queen of Cups as feeling
    understood from the inside, held steady, and offered with care. She is
    often read as the empathic person who can sit with another person's
    emotions without being swept away, or as an inner stance of emotional
    depth and intuition. The sealed cup is taken as a sign that her feelings
    are kept as much as shared, and her calm as the result of practice rather
    than absence of feeling. Many readers use the card as a role or a
    quality to draw on, rather than for a particular person.
invitationPrompt: What would it be like to let yourself feel something fully
    without having to do anything about it yet?
