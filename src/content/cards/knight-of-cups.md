name: Knight of Cups
symbols:
    - element: A knight in silver armor on a pale horse, both in profile and
          moving to the right at a slow, measured walk
      note: The Knight is the rank defined by direction, but this one is not
          charging. The horse steps forward with its head lowered and its
          rider sits calmly, so the pursuit here is steady and unhurried.
    - element: A golden cup held out level in one outstretched hand, at arm's
          length in front of him
      note: The cup is carried as an offering and not a weapon, held out
          carefully as if its contents might spill. It is the suit's emblem
          made into the Knight's quest.
    - element: A winged helmet and wings on the heels of his armor
      note: Wings on helmet and heel are a familiar sign of a messenger, and
          they give the Knight a light, swift character despite the slow
          gait. He is often read as someone carrying feeling from one place
          or person to another.
    - element: A tunic worn over the armor, patterned with red fish and
          wavy lines
      note: Fish and waves bring the suit's element onto the Knight's own
          body. He wears the feeling he is carrying, and it is decorative
          rather than defensive.
    - element: A narrow river winding through a flat yellow plain, with a
          rust-colored crag at the right edge
      note: The water runs alongside the path and not across it, so the
          Knight follows it rather than fighting it. The plain is open, and
          the crag rises off to the side of his path rather than across it.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats the Knight of Cups as the pursuit of
    a feeling, the stage where the suit's energy is committed to and acted on.
    He is often read as the romantic, the bearer of an invitation or an offer,
    and the person who follows their heart with real conviction. Readers apply
    the card to a proposal, an act of devotion, or an idealistic project taken
    up with care. The Knight's strength is follow-through, and his risk is
    tunnel vision, where a beautiful idea of something is followed more
    closely than the thing itself.
invitationPrompt: What are you carrying carefully toward someone or something,
    and how will you know when you have arrived?
