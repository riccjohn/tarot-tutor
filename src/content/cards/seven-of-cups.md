name: Seven of Cups
symbols:
    - element: A dark silhouetted figure seen from behind at the lower left,
          one arm stretched out toward the cups
      note: The figure is a flat black shape with no face, features, or
          clothing detail, so the viewer takes the figure's position in
          front of the choices. The raised hand is read as reaching, or as
          hesitating, since it has not yet closed on anything.
    - element: Seven golden cups floating on a bank of grey clouds, each
          holding a different object
      note: The cups are the only objects in the scene with nothing under
          them but cloud, and they are arranged as a spread of options
          rather than a sequence. That is the number's point, a crowd of
          possibilities with no obvious ranking among them.
    - element: A curly-haired head in the top left cup, and a shrouded
          figure under a red glowing outline in the top middle cup
      note: One is a face and one is a covered figure, so the cups offer
          both a person and something concealed. Readers often take them as
          the pull of another person and the pull of a mystery.
    - element: A coiled snake in the top right cup, and a winged dragon in
          the lower right cup
      note: These are the cups with an element of danger. They sit at the far
          edge of the arrangement, and the same golden cups that hold gifts
          hold these too, so nothing on the surface tells them apart.
    - element: A castle on a tall rock in the lower left cup, a heap of
          jewels and a hanging chain in the next, and a laurel wreath in
          the fifth, its cup marked with a skull
      note: Power, wealth, and victory, the three familiar temptations,
          each drawn as an object heaped in a cup. The wreath sits over a
          skull, a traditional reminder that success has its own
          complications.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a crowd of possibilities
    and the difficulty of telling which are real. The cups float on cloud, so
    each choice looks equally vivid, and the figure in front of them has not
    yet chosen. Many readers associate the card with fantasy, wishful thinking,
    or an overload of options, where imagining has become easier than
    committing. The number is a test of commitment, and here what is being
    tested is discernment, meaning which of these images would still be
    something the reader wants once they have stopped glowing.
invitationPrompt: Which of the options in front of you feels most vivid, and
    what would it look like on an ordinary Tuesday?
