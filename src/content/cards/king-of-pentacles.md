name: King of Pentacles
symbols:
    - element: A king seated upright and facing forward on a dark throne, in
          a red hood and a wreath of red flowers over a small crown
      note: The pose is the King's usual one, seated and looking directly out
          at the viewer. The flowers in the crown belong to the suit's
          garden and bring growth up to his head, so his rule is that of a
          cultivator rather than a conqueror.
    - element: A robe covered in green vine leaves and bunches of red grapes
          on a dark ground, worn over a steel armored boot
      note: The vines grow over his clothing as they do over the throne,
          which reads as wealth that has been long maintained. The armored
          foot showing beneath is a reminder that he can defend it.
    - element: A large pentacle resting on one knee under his hand, and a
          gold orb-topped scepter in the other
      note: He holds the coin and the scepter at once, so ownership and
          command are held together. His hand on the coin is calm, and
          neither the coin nor the rule seems in any danger of slipping away.
    - element: Bull heads carved on the back and arms of the throne
      note: Conventionally read as the suit's earthy, stubborn strength. The
          bulls are set into the structure of his seat, so patience and
          endurance are part of what he sits on.
    - element: A stone castle with red-roofed towers at the right, and a
          garden wall with vines, flowers, and green foliage around the
          throne
      note: The King's holdings are built, walled, and in order. Unlike the
          Queen's open garden, his territory is defined and defended, so
          his authority reads as outward-facing management of a lasting
          estate.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as mastery of the material
    world, with the security of a person who has built something durable and
    now looks after it. As the King, he acts outward, and in Pentacles that
    means providing for others, managing resources with care, and giving
    steady, practical counsel. He is read as a mentor or a business
    figure, or as a stance in which what has been earned is used to make
    others secure. The rank's hazard is present here too, since the same
    solidity can turn into a wish for control or an attachment to
    comfort, and readers weigh which side of the card speaks to them.
invitationPrompt: What have you built that others now depend on, and how do
    you want to look after it?
