name: Three of Pentacles
symbols:
    - element: Three pentacles carved into the stone of a pointed arch, two
          side by side and one above, set around a small central circle
      note: The coins are part of the building itself, cut into the fabric
          of the church rather than held by anyone. The suit's emblem here
          is the work the figures are gathered around.
    - element: A stonemason in a grey tunic, seen from behind, standing on a
          wooden bench and working the stone with a tool in one hand
      note: He is the one doing the labor and the only figure raised up, at
          the height the work requires. His back is to the viewer, so the
          card puts the craft in front of the face.
    - element: A monk in grey and a figure in a long orange cloak spotted
          with red, standing to the right, the monk holding an unrolled
          sheet of plans
      note: They are conventionally read as the ones who commissioned or
          planned the work. The sheet ties what is being built to a design
          drawn beforehand, and both figures look toward the mason.
    - element: A central stone pillar between the mason and the two figures,
          with arches springing from its top
      note: The pillar separates the workers physically, though the arch
          above all three joins them. The three people stand in one
          structure while occupying different roles.
    - element: A dark vaulted interior with a flagged floor and a brick wall
          at the top corners
      note: The setting is enclosed and solid, with all three figures inside
          one structure, which fits a card about work done within a shared
          framework.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as collaboration in which
    each person brings a different skill to a shared piece of work. The mason
    builds, the monk holds the plans, and the third figure looks on as the
    one the work is for, and none of the three could finish it alone. The Three's turn toward
    complication appears here as a third party entering the pairing and
    changing it, and in Pentacles that complication is usually productive,
    since the work becomes visible and takes shape. It is often read as
    recognition of skill and as the point where a private effort becomes a
    shared one.
invitationPrompt: Whose skill are you relying on right now, and whose might
    be relying on yours?
