---
name: Queen of Wands
symbols:
    - element: A seated figure in a long yellow gown with a grey mantle,
          wearing a crown of small flowers and looking out to the side
      note: The Queen is seated and composed, at ease in the suit's landscape.
          The rank is defined by that ease, since the energy of Wands has
          already been learned and is no longer being chased.
    - element: A tall budding staff held upright in one hand, and a large
          sunflower held out in the other
      note: The wand is the suit's emblem, and the sunflower sits beside it as
          a second one. The bloom is often read as warmth and outward growth,
          drive that has become generous rather than restless.
    - element: A throne with a lion's head carved on each armrest, and a tall
          banner behind it showing two red lions and more sunflowers
      note: Lions are a conventional image of confidence and fire. They are
          carved into the seat and stitched into the backdrop, so the Queen
          is surrounded by them and not compelled by them.
    - element: A black cat sitting upright on the step at her feet, facing the
          viewer
      note: The cat is the only figure that looks out of the card. It is
          usually read as independence or instinct, and it is calm rather than
          startled, like the Queen herself.
    - element: A pale blue sky with a hint of yellow desert and pointed hills
          at the left and grey ground at the right
      note: The landscape is the same warm, dry country as the other Wands
          courts. It is kept low and small behind her, letting the throne carry
          the composition.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as Wands energy turned
    warm and settled, a person or stance of confident vitality that shapes
    what is around it mostly by presence. The Queen has moved past the
    Knight's pursuit and lives inside her drive, so it shows up as charisma,
    generosity, and steadiness more than force. The reading is not tied to
    gender and is often used for a role or an inner stance. In Wands the
    Queen is the passion that has matured into confidence.
invitationPrompt: Where do you already feel at home in your own energy, and
    what does that look like when you are not trying to prove it?
