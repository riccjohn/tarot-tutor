name: Eight of Cups
symbols:
    - element: A figure in a red cloak and red boots, seen from behind,
          walking away from the viewer with a long staff in one hand
      note: The figure is already in motion and facing the other direction,
          so the card shows the departure and not the deciding. The staff and
          boots mark it as a journey on foot, and the bright red is the
          strongest color on an otherwise blue-grey card.
    - element: Eight golden cups arranged in two neat groups on the near
          ground, five in a row along the bottom and three stacked above them,
          with a gap left between the groups
      note: The cups are lined up in good order and none is broken or spilled.
          What is being left behind is complete, and the gap in the stack
          marks the space the figure has stepped out of.
    - element: A large moon in a blue-grey sky, with a crescent lying across
          a full yellow disc and a calm, closed-eyed face
      note: The moon is conventionally tied to inner life and tides. Its
          crescent sits inside the full disc, so it shows both a light part
          and a dark part at once. Its eyes are closed, and readers often
          take it as a quiet, watching presence for the departure.
    - element: A river running across the middle of the scene, with rocks in
          the foreground and steep green and black hills beyond
      note: The figure heads along the water toward the mountains, away from
          the cups and into rougher ground. The landscape ahead is harder and
          emptier than the tidy row behind, and nothing in it is promised.
    - element: The roman numeral VIII at the top, above the moon
      note: The numeral sits over the sky and not over the cups, so the
          card's number is tied to the moving figure and the moon rather than
          to the objects left behind.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a deliberate walking
    away from something that no longer satisfies. The cups are all still
    standing, so the departure is not a response to a collapse. It is a choice
    to leave something that was working on paper. Readers link it to leaving a
    job, a relationship, or a habit that has stopped feeling like enough, and
    to the mix of loss and relief that comes with that. Where the figure is
    heading is left open, and the card pictures the leaving more than the
    arriving.
invitationPrompt: What have you been carrying that looks complete from the
    outside but no longer feels like yours?
