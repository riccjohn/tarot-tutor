name: Six of Cups
symbols:
    - element: An older child in a red hood and blue tunic bending toward a
          smaller child, offering a cup of flowers
      note: The gift passes from the taller figure to the shorter one, so the
          Six's exchange is drawn literally, with someone helping someone
          else. The children's differing heights are often read as two ages
          or two stages, one handing something across to the other.
    - element: Six golden cups, each holding green leaves and a white
          star-shaped flower, four in a row across the foreground, one on a
          stone pedestal, and one in the offered hand
      note: The suit's vessels are filled with flowers rather than liquid,
          which turns the feeling in the cups into something that can be
          picked, carried, and given away. The flowers are the same in every
          cup, so the gift is repeated rather than singled out.
    - element: A stone pedestal at the left carved with a white X-shaped cross
          on a shield
      note: A civic or ceremonial marker that places the scene inside an
          established, cared-for place. The cup on top is one of the six, so
          the emblem sits in the same visual family as the gifts.
    - element: A stone tower and a low house with a grey slate roof, behind
          the children
      note: The buildings are old and solid, and the courtyard they enclose is
          quiet. The setting suggests a place that has been lived in for a
          long time, and it is commonly tied to the card's association with
          childhood and memory.
    - element: A small figure in blue walking away on the far left, near
          the tower steps
      note: A distant adult goes about the day while the exchange between the
          children takes place in the foreground, so the scene is a small,
          unwatched moment.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as generosity or memory,
    feeling passed from one person to another. Many readers link it to
    nostalgia, to childhood, or to an old kindness that returns, and it sits
    comfortably with reunion and the simple pleasure of sharing something. The
    Five's strain has eased, and what moves between the two children is the
    mark of the number. Readers differ on whether the card looks back toward
    the past or outward toward a gift being made now, and the image supports
    both.
invitationPrompt: What is something small you could hand to someone today, and
    what does it remind you of?
