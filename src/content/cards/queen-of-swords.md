name: Queen of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A seated figure in profile facing right, wearing a gold crown
          shaped with butterfly-like wings and a cloak patterned with grey
          clouds on blue
      note: The Queen sits in the sky's own pattern, her cloak dressed in the
          clouds behind her. She is at home in the suit's element, not
          visiting it.
    - element: A long straight sword held upright in one hand, the point
          well above her head and the hilt resting near her lap
      note: The blade is vertical and steady, and it is held rather than
          swung. Read as clear thinking kept in reach and used as a
          boundary, not drawn against anyone.
    - element: The other hand raised at shoulder height, open with the palm
          turned up and forward
      note: An open hand next to a drawn sword. It is a gesture of address,
          speaking or offering, which pairs statement with edge.
    - element: A grey carved throne with a small human face at the centre of
          its seat-back, winged forms below it, and a scrolled arm at the
          left
      note: The throne is elaborate and heavy while the Queen sits lightly on
          it. The winged forms tie the seat to the air, in keeping with
          the element usually given to the suit.
    - element: A bank of white clouds behind her, one small bird high in the
          pale blue sky, and a few dark trees along a red path at the lower
          left
      note: The setting is high and open, and the bird is the only other
          living thing in it. The Queen's view is wide, and the ground she
          looks over is far below.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats the Queen of Swords as clear
    thinking settled into a person or an inner stance, with her boundaries
    stated and her judgment considered. The upright sword and the open hand
    together are often taken to mean honesty offered without cruelty, and
    directness that has already been thought through. As a person she is
    someone who says the plain thing, or who can hold a hard fact steady for
    others without softening it into something else. It is a stance a reader
    of any gender might recognise in themselves at some times and not at
    others.
invitationPrompt: Where would a plain, considered statement serve you better
    today than a careful silence?
