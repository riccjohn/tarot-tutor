---
name: Five of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A red-haired figure in a green tunic with red sleeves in the
          foreground, smirking to the left, with two swords gathered against
          their shoulder and a third held point-down in the other hand
      note: The figure holds three of the five swords and looks pleased. On
          this card the winner is the one carrying the blades, and the
          expression asks whether what they carry was worth taking.
    - element: Two swords lying on the ground at the lower left, blades
          pointing in different directions
      note: The two dropped blades belong to the other side. They are
          discarded rather than broken, so the conflict has ended without
          either side being destroyed.
    - element: Two smaller figures walking away toward the water, one in a
          yellow tunic with a red cloak and one in yellow, both facing away
          from the viewer
      note: Read as the ones who lost the argument, or who chose to leave it.
          Their backs are turned, so what they feel is not shown. The
          viewer sees the winner's face and only the others' departure.
    - element: A flat grey shoreline and an expanse of choppy blue water
          beyond the figures
      note: The setting is open and empty of any third party. There is no
          audience for the conflict, and nothing in the landscape is on
          anyone's side.
    - element: Ragged grey clouds streaking diagonally across a pale blue sky,
          with the numeral V at the top
      note: The torn edges of the clouds echo the tension on the ground. The
          numeral marks the midpoint of the ten, where the earlier steadiness
          is under strain.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a conflict won at a
    cost. Someone has got their way, and the card asks what that victory
    left behind — the two who walked off, the swords on the ground. The Five
    is the number where settled arrangements meet friction, and in Swords the
    friction is an argument rather than a competition or a shortage. Readers
    differ on whether the figure in front is the one to identify with or the
    one to be wary of. Either way, the card points to the gap between being
    right and having something worth keeping.
invitationPrompt: If you got the outcome you wanted from a disagreement today,
    what would you be leaving behind to get it?
