---
name: Knight of Pentacles
symbols:
    - element: A rider in a pale steel helmet with a green leafy plume and
          light armor over a red-striped tunic, seen in profile
      note: The Knight is dressed for work, not for battle, with no sword or
          lance on show. The green plume is the same foliage the horse wears,
          tying the rider to the land.
    - element: A single pentacle held up in one red-gloved hand, level with
          the rider's chest
      note: He holds the coin out at arm's length and studies it, as the Page
          does. Unlike the Page, he is mounted and equipped, so the coin has
          become the object of a quest.
    - element: A heavy black horse standing with its front legs planted, head
          lowered, wearing a red bridle and a sprig of green in its forelock
      note: The horse stands still, unlike the charging or walking horses of
          the other Knights. The rank's usual forward drive is held in
          check here, and that stillness is what this Knight is known for.
    - element: Plowed fields of red furrows running across the middle
          distance, under a low green hill
      note: The land has already been worked and is laid out in long, even
          rows. It suggests that the Knight's kind of progress is measured
          in tilled ground, at a steady pace.
    - element: A flat yellow sky and a few dark trees on the far right
          horizon
      note: The background is bare, so the card is about the horse, the
          rider, and the coin. Nothing is racing toward the frame or away from
          it.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as the Knight's pursuit
    turned patient, with steady, methodical progress toward a practical goal.
    Where the other Knights charge, this one is still, and his commitment
    shows in how carefully he keeps going. He is read as reliability and
    thoroughness, the person who follows a plan through to the end. The
    rank's cost is also present here, since the same steadiness can harden
    into slowness or a refusal to change course when the plan has stopped
    fitting, and readers often ask which of those the card is pointing to.
invitationPrompt: Where in your life is a slow, steady approach doing better
    than a fast one would, and where might it be holding you back?
