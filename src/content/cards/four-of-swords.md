name: Four of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A figure in armour lying on their back on a long yellow stone
          slab, eyes closed, hands pressed together upright over the chest
      note: The figure is horizontal, still, and entirely at ease; nothing on
          the card is being fought or carried. Read here as rest taken on
          purpose, with the hands held together as a gesture of quiet rather
          than effort.
    - element: Three swords hung point-down on the wall above the figure, side
          by side and evenly spaced
      note: Three blades are put away in plain view, off the figure's body
          and not in use. They are stored rather than discarded, which fits
          a pause more than an ending.
    - element: A fourth sword carved or laid along the front face of the slab
          beneath the figure, running horizontally
      note: This blade lies flat where the other three hang upright. It is
          the fourth sword and the only one at the figure's own level, so
          the pause is built into the ground the figure rests on.
    - element: A stained-glass window in the upper left showing two figures,
          one standing and one kneeling, in red, blue, yellow, and green
      note: The one source of colour on an otherwise grey and cream card.
          Often read as a quiet turn toward care, comfort, or help outside
          the mind's own arguments.
    - element: The numeral IV in the upper right corner, beside a hanging
          staff or banner pole
      note: The number of the first stable structure. Here the structure is
          a room built for stopping.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as deliberate rest — a
    stretch of quiet in which the mind is allowed to stop after conflict or
    effort. The swords are not gone; they are hung on the wall where they can
    be seen, which is often taken to mean that the problem is still there
    but does not need to be worked on this minute. This is the number Four
    in its steadying form, a structure built for stopping. Some readers
    push it further and see it as the recovery a hard stretch requires
    before anything else can be decided.
invitationPrompt: Where in your day could you put something down without
    solving it first?
