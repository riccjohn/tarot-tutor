name: Nine of Pentacles
symbols:
    - element: A woman alone in a long cream robe patterned with small red
          flowers, standing upright with one hand resting on a coin and the
          other raised
      note: Only one figure appears in the whole garden. The robe is
          elaborate and the posture relaxed, which is the Nine's lone figure
          shown at ease instead of on guard.
    - element: A hooded falcon perched on her raised, gloved hand
      note: A hooded bird is a trained one, held still by the wearer's
          authority. It is often read as discipline, something wild that has
          been brought into partnership, and it adds a second kind of
          mastery to the wealth around her.
    - element: A vineyard of green leaves and heavy red grape clusters behind
          her on both sides
      note: The grapes are ripe and full, so the ground has already produced.
          The card shows the harvest as present, not promised.
    - element: Nine pentacles set among the vines, six clustered at the left
          and three at the right
      note: The coins are part of the garden, growing with the grapes as in
          the Seven. They are hers in the sense of the land they belong to,
          and she stands among them and not over them.
    - element: A small snail on the path at the bottom left, under a plain
          yellow sky with a tree on each side and a house at the far right
      note: Conventionally read as slowness, the pace at which this garden
          was made. The house on the horizon is close enough to suggest home
          without being part of the scene.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as self-sufficiency,
    comfort earned through one's own work. The Nine is a moment of taking
    stock, and here the stock is good, with the vines full, the coins
    around her, and no one else is needed in the frame. The falcon and the
    garden together suggest independence built up with patience. It is often
    read as satisfaction in what one has made, and readers sometimes hear a
    quieter note in it, that a well-kept solitude can be a choice and not only
    a circumstance.
invitationPrompt: What have you made that you can enjoy without needing
    anyone's approval of it?
