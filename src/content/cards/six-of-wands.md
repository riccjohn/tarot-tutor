name: Six of Wands
symbols:
    - element: A rider in profile in a red cloak, seated on a white horse and
          facing right, with a green wreath of leaves on his head
      note: The wreath is a conventional mark of victory. The rider is the
          only figure on horseback, raised above everyone else on the card.
    - element: One staff held upright in the rider's hand, with a
          second wreath tied near its top by a red ribbon
      note: The largest wand carries the prize. Its wreath echoes the one on
          the rider's head, so the honour appears twice, once worn and once
          carried.
    - element: Five more staffs standing upright behind and around the rider,
          held by figures on foot at the left edge
      note: The other wands are not aimed at anyone. Six is the number
          where the Five's crossed and clashing staffs have been lifted
          into order, and everyone now holds theirs the same way.
    - element: A group of onlookers on foot at the left edge, only their
          faces and raised hands visible
      note: The win is witnessed. The scene depends on a crowd, so the card
          reads as recognition given by others as much as an achievement
          held alone.
    - element: A white horse with a green and red patterned cloth and a
          red bridle, its head lowered toward the right edge
      note: The horse is calm rather than charging, so the movement on the
          card is a procession. It is a slow, ceremonial advance, not a race.
    - element: A rolling green hill under a plain blue sky
      note: The ground is smooth and open, with no obstacle in sight. The
          effort belongs to the past and the road ahead looks clear.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as effort recognized in
    public, a win acknowledged and shared by those who watched the work
    happen. The Five's scattered contest has resolved into a procession, and
    the rider is carried along by the attention of others as much as by their
    own ability. In Wands, the suit of will and ambition, the Six is success
    that is seen. It sits alongside the question of how much the recognition
    matters compared with the work.
invitationPrompt: Whose acknowledgment of your effort do you find yourself
    waiting for, and what would you say to yourself in its absence?
