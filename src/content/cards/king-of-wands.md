name: King of Wands
symbols:
    - element: A seated figure in profile, facing left, in a red robe with a
          green collar and a gold chain, wearing a gold crown over a red cap
      note: Unlike the Queen, who looks out of her card, the King sits turned
          toward something beyond the picture's edge. It reads as a ruler
          whose attention is on what he directs.
    - element: A tall staff held upright in one hand beside him, with several
          green leaves budding along its length
      note: The wand is grounded on the step beside the throne and taller
          than the King. It is the suit's emblem, held as a fixed reference
          point more than as an instrument in motion.
    - element: A long mantle over the throne, patterned with dark curling
          shapes, spilling down the right side of the card
      note: The pattern of dark curls also appears on the Page and the
          Knight. It is often read as fire's emblem, so the rank that
          completes the suit's court wears the same mark it began with.
    - element: A tall orange banner behind the throne with black lions and
          curling, tail-biting creatures stitched into it
      note: Lions again point to authority, and the tail-biting shapes are
          often read as cycles closing. Together they frame a ruler who
          sits at the end of a long stretch of the suit.
    - element: A small black salamander-like lizard on the grey step at his
          feet
      note: The lizard is a conventional fire creature, a small living version
          of the pattern on his mantle. It sits at the foot of the throne,
          the one animal in the picture at ease beside the King.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as Wands energy fully
    mastered and directed outward, leadership through vision, decisiveness,
    and the confidence to say where the work is going. The King acts on what
    the Queen understands, deciding and taking responsibility for what
    follows. He is a mentor or a person with real command of a field, and in
    Wands that command comes from drive rather than from procedure. The rank
    carries its own risk, since authority that no longer listens can harden,
    and the strongest version keeps some of the Page's curiosity.
invitationPrompt: Where are you being asked to set the direction today, and
    who else does that direction touch?
