name: The Tower
symbols:
    - element: A tall grey tower standing on a jagged rocky peak, with
          flames coming out of its narrow windows
      note: The tower is a built structure, tall and narrow with a small
          footprint on the rock, and the card is about how that structure
          holds up. The fire from its windows shows the disturbance
          reaching the inside.
    - element: A yellow crown at the tower's top, knocked off and tilting
          away as a zigzag bolt of lightning strikes from the upper right
      note: The crown is conventionally read as a claim to certainty or
          height that the structure carried. Its being dislodged is the
          card's turning point, coming from outside the tower instead of
          within it.
    - element: Two figures falling headfirst on either side of the tower,
          one in a red cloak on the left and one in blue on the right
      note: Both figures are leaving the structure and have no footing
          in the picture. Their symmetrical placement shows the event
          touching everyone who stood in the tower, not one person.
    - element: Small yellow flame-shaped drops scattered through the black
          sky on both sides
      note: The drops are usually read as sparks or as falling embers. They
          fill the empty space evenly, so the whole scene reads as one
          moment of disruption.
    - element: Grey clouds against a black sky, with the numeral XVI at the
          top
      note: The dark field makes the lit tower and bolt the only
          bright objects. There is no ground shown beneath the figures,
          which leaves open what is waiting below.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading takes this card as sudden upheaval, a
    foundation that turns out to be less stable than it looked, and the
    clearing that follows. Written plainly, it is about a break with
    something built on a shaky base. In the sequence it follows the Devil, a
    structure held in place, and it shows one coming apart, which some
    readers take as a release as much as a loss. What the break makes
    room for is not drawn on the card.
invitationPrompt: Which structure in your life today feels most solid, and
    what is it resting on?
