---
name: Six of Pentacles
symbols:
    - element: A standing figure in a red robe and cap, one hand dropping
          coins toward a kneeling figure below
      note: The giver is the tallest thing on the card and the gift moves
          downward across a real difference in position. Whether that
          difference is generosity or hierarchy is part of what the card
          leaves open.
    - element: A pair of brass scales held up in the figure's other hand,
          with a pan hanging from each end of the beam
      note: The scales are the card's second claim next to the coins. They
          suggest that the giving is measured, that someone has weighed what
          is owed and what is shared.
    - element: Two figures kneeling at the giver's feet, one in a pale
          yellow cloak reaching up with open hands and one in a blue cloak
          with a hand raised toward the giver's leg
      note: Both are receivers, and both face the giver. There is a patch on
          the blue cloak. The card shows two people needing and one person
          able to give, so there is give and take in the scene, though not
          equally distributed.
    - element: Six coins spread across the upper half of the card, three down
          the left, one at the center, and two at the right, above the figures
      note: The pips hang over the scene like the fund the giving draws on.
          The few coins in the giver's hand are a small part of what is shown
          above, so the exchange is between people and the wealth stays
          overhead.
    - element: A strip of green shrubs and the roofs of a small town at the
          right edge, behind the kneeling figures
      note: The exchange takes place at the edge of a settled place, and it
          is the community's business as much as a private one.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as the balance of giving
    and receiving, and how fairly it is shared. After the Five's shortfall,
    the Six is a first step back toward order, in which resources move
    between people. The scales and the coins carry two questions at once,
    what is being given and how it is being weighed. Readers see it as
    generosity received or offered, and also as a prompt to notice who holds
    the power in an exchange and how kindly it is used.
invitationPrompt: Where are you on this card today, giving, receiving, or
    watching the scales, and does that feel right?
