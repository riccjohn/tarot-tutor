name: King of Cups
symbols:
    - element: A crowned man seated upright and facing forward on a grey
          throne with scrolled sides, in a blue robe under a green cloak
          with a red mantle
      note: The frontal, symmetrical pose is the King's mark, a ruler who
          looks out and takes responsibility for what is in front of him.
          The blue robe is the color of water, held together under the heavier
          green cloak.
    - element: A golden cup in one hand and a short golden scepter in the
          other
      note: The cup is the suit's feeling and the scepter is authority, and
          he holds both at once. Neither is raised or brandished. He carries
          them at rest, as tools of office rather than as displays.
    - element: A small fish pendant hanging on a chain at his chest
      note: A fish worn as a badge of office is the sea's emblem taken up
          into the King's own identity. It is a small detail, and it reads as
          feeling he has learned to wear openly and carry as part of his
          role.
    - element: A throne set on a flat stone slab directly in the sea, with
          waves rolling on every side
      note: Where the Queen sits at the shore, the King sits in the water
          itself, with his seat in the middle of the waves. The sea is all
          around him and he rules from within it, calm while it moves.
    - element: A leaping fish at the left and a ship with a red sail at the
          right, both small against the waves
      note: One creature and one vessel, at either edge of the scene. Life
          in the water and travel across it both go on around him, and the
          King's stillness is what lets them move freely.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats the King of Cups as mature, steady
    feeling, emotional command that is calm without being cold. He sits in
    the open sea and does not react to every wave. Readers apply the card to a
    mentor, a counselor, or anyone who can hold strong feelings alongside a
    responsibility to others, and also to an inner stance of composure that
    still feels things. The rank carries its own hazard, since composure can
    harden into distance, and whether this King is generous or withholding is
    something readers have long disagreed about.
invitationPrompt: Where are you being asked to stay steady while things move
    around you, and what does that steadiness cost or give you?
