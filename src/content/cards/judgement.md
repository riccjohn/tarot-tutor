---
name: Judgement
symbols:
    - element: A winged figure with curled yellow hair and dark red wings,
          emerging from grey clouds at the top of the card and sounding a
          long gold trumpet
      note: The trumpet is the card's central act, a call aimed straight
          down at the figures below. It is conventionally read as a summons
          to respond to something, not a verdict passed on anyone.
    - element: A white square banner with a red equal-armed cross hanging
          from the trumpet
      note: The cross is a plain, balanced shape, its four arms equal. It is
          the one geometric mark against the surrounding clouds and
          figures, and readers often take it as the call's source or
          authority.
    - element: A group of grey, unclothed figures standing in open
          rectangular boxes in the lower half, arms raised toward the sky
      note: Every figure faces up at the trumpet and reaches toward it. The
          open boxes read as places the figures are leaving, so the card
          shows people answering a call and getting up rather than being
          judged in place.
    - element: A man, a woman, and a child at the front of the group, the
          woman with long yellow hair and the child seen from behind
      note: The three foreground figures cover a range of ages and are turned
          in different directions. They are answering the same sound
          from different positions.
    - element: Snow-capped mountains and a wide stretch of water behind
          the figures, under a pale blue sky
      note: The landscape is open and calm, with no hazard drawn in it. It
          sets the calling in a wide, quiet place rather than an enclosed
          one.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a calling and a
    review, looking back over what has been lived and deciding to answer
    it. Placed after the Sun, it turns clarity into a response. The raised
    arms and upturned faces are commonly read as readiness to be
    counted and to begin again from what the past has shown. The
    trumpet asks, and what the answer is belongs to the person hearing it.
invitationPrompt: If you looked back over the past year, what is the one
    thing you feel called to respond to?
