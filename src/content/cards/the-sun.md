---
name: The Sun
symbols:
    - element: A large yellow sun with a calm human face, alternating
          straight and wavy rays, and the numeral XIX above it
      note: The sun looks straight out at the viewer with an even,
          unworried expression. The mix of straight and wavy rays is
          conventionally read as light that both reaches and warms.
    - element: A small unclothed child with a wreath of red flowers and a
          red feather, arms spread wide, sitting on a horse
      note: The child is drawn at ease, with no reins and no clothing.
          The open-armed pose is the card's most direct picture of
          openness, and it is often read as trust in the moment.
    - element: A white horse standing calmly, facing right, with no saddle
          or bridle shown
      note: The horse is the calm, large animal that carries the child. It
          stands in for a steadiness the child does not have to provide.
    - element: A long orange-red banner streaming from behind the child in
          the wind
      note: The banner is the only strongly saturated red in the scene
          and the only thing visibly moving. It gives the card its energy and
          leads the eye upward toward the sun.
    - element: A row of tall sunflowers growing in front of a grey brick
          wall
      note: The flowers turn toward the sun, the plainest link between
          sky and ground on the card. The wall sets a boundary, but it is
          low enough that the flowers rise above it.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading takes this card as clarity and warmth,
    things seen as they are. Following the Moon's uncertainty, it is the
    row's turn into daylight, and nothing on the card is hidden. The
    child, the horse, and the flowers each look toward the sun or ride
    easily beneath it, and readers often take that as simple confidence,
    the kind that does not need to be argued for.
invitationPrompt: Where in your day could you let things be exactly as
    plain as they look?
