name: The Chariot
symbols:
    - element: A young armoured figure stands upright in a stone chariot beneath a blue canopy scattered with white stars, holding a short wand and wearing a star-topped wreath
      note: He stands rather than sits, and there are no reins in his hands. Conventionally read as direction held by will, with the canopy tying the movement to something larger than himself.
    - element: Two sphinxes crouched at the front of the chariot, one black and one white, both in striped headcloths
      note: They face forward together and neither is shown pulling. Often read as opposing forces, such as dark and light or impulse and restraint, that the driver holds in balance instead of subduing.
    - element: Crescent moons on both shoulders of his armour, with square and geometric marks on his belt and skirt
      note: The moons are worn as part of the armour itself. Often tied to intuition carried into action, and to symbols the driver has taken on as his own.
    - element: A winged red emblem on the front of the chariot, showing a small disc with wings above a shield
      note: The winged disc is a sign long associated with the sun. Often read as purpose that carries the vehicle, and it sits directly beneath him.
    - element: A walled city with red-roofed towers behind the chariot, and a strip of blue water between the city and the ground
      note: The city and river sit behind him rather than ahead. Read as the settled world he is leaving or has mastered enough to steer beyond.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading takes this card as directed will, forward movement that comes from holding opposing pulls in balance. The driver has no reins in his hands, and the black and white sphinxes stay together by his direction rather than by force. As the end of the first row of the majors, it is the outer world mastered enough to steer. What is worth driving toward is left for the reader to decide.
invitationPrompt: What are the two pulls in your day, and what would it take to move with both instead of picking one?
