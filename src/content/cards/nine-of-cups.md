name: Nine of Cups
symbols:
    - element: A stout man seated on a wooden bench, facing forward, wearing a
          red cap with a red plume and a white robe with dark stripes
      note: He is the only figure on the card and he looks directly out at
          the viewer with a small smile. The direct gaze and the relaxed
          posture make this the card's picture of someone who is comfortable
          being seen.
    - element: His arms crossed over his belly, hands resting on his opposite
          arms
      note: The crossed arms are the same gesture as the Four's, but here they
          read as satisfaction rather than withdrawal. The same posture means
          something different when the figure is looking out and not in.
    - element: Nine golden cups arranged in a shallow arc on a curved shelf
          behind him
      note: The cups form a neat line, all upright and all in view, like
          possessions on display. They are behind his head rather than in
          his hands, so what he has is his to look at and is not in use.
    - element: A blue cloth draped in folds behind and around the bench, open
          at the front
      note: The drapery curves around him like a small stage, framing him
          and the cups as one enclosed space. It also closes the scene off
          from any landscape or weather, so the card reads as a private,
          finished moment.
    - element: A flat yellow background and yellow ground, with the roman
          numeral IX at the top
      note: A single bright color fills everything outside the blue cloth,
          so the card has no horizon, no distance, and no other people. It is
          a scene of one person and what he has gathered.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as contentment, the feeling
    of having what was wanted. The man sits at ease in front of nine cups and
    looks pleased with the arrangement. Readers often call it the wish
    card, meaning satisfaction in a hope come true, and others read it as
    comfort or pleasure enjoyed in private. The Nine is a moment of taking
    stock, and here the stock is favorable. Whether that satisfaction is deep
    or a little smug is a question the card raises without answering, and
    readers have taken it both ways.
invitationPrompt: What do you already have that you have not yet let yourself
    enjoy?
