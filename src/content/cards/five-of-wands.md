name: Five of Wands
symbols:
    - element: Five young men in mismatched, brightly coloured tunics, each
          holding a wooden staff, on a patch of bare ground
      note: Five is one more than the settled Four, and each figure here is
          a separate element. No two are dressed alike, so the crowd reads
          as a group of individuals rather than two opposing sides.
    - element: Five staffs raised at different angles, crossing one another
          in the middle of the group
      note: The wands do not line up, and no one staff is aimed at a single
          target. The friction is scattered, with everyone pushing in a
          direction of their own.
    - element: Small green leaves still sprouting from the ends and sides of
          the staffs
      note: The wands are living wood, as in every card of the suit. The
          effort on display is energetic rather than destructive.
    - element: Expressions turned upward or sideways, no figure looking at a
          wound or a fallen opponent
      note: Nobody on the card is down or bleeding. The picture is closer to
          a scramble or a rehearsal than to a battle, which is why it is
          often read as competition, not violence.
    - element: A flat, empty field of brown and green ground under a plain
          blue sky
      note: There is no shelter, spectator, or prize in view. The setting
          leaves the contest with no stated purpose, only the jostling itself.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as friction between
    people who want different things, a jostling of ideas, egos, and plans
    where no one is yet in charge. Because nobody on the card is hurt, it is
    often read as competition or productive disagreement more than open
    hostility. In Wands, the suit of drive, the Five is what happens when
    several drives meet in one place. It is uncomfortable and not final, and
    the cards that follow move past it.
invitationPrompt: Where in your day are several good ideas competing for the
    same space, and what would each of them need in order to coexist?
