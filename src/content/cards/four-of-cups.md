name: Four of Cups
symbols:
    - element: A young man seated cross-legged on a grassy rise with his arms
          folded across his chest, his back against a tree trunk
      note: Everything about the posture is closed and enclosed, from the
          folded arms to the crossed legs to the tree at his back. It is the
          Four's stability drawn as a person, settled in place and not
          reaching for anything.
    - element: Three golden cups standing in a row on the grass in front of
          him
      note: The cups sit within easy reach but he looks past them, toward
          the ground. What he already has is arranged neatly in his own
          territory, and the card leaves open whether he is content with it
          or has stopped noticing it.
    - element: A hand emerging from a small grey cloud at the left edge,
          offering a fourth golden cup
      note: The hand is the same device that presents the Ace of Cups. Here it
          holds the cup out at the edge of the frame, where he is not looking
          at it. It is read as a new offer arriving and being unseen.
    - element: A large tree with a broad trunk and a leafy canopy overhead,
          filling the upper half of the card
      note: The tree is tall, still, and rooted, and it frames the seated
          figure as part of a quiet, sheltered scene. Nothing in the
          landscape is moving.
    - element: A clear blue sky with the roman numeral IV at the top, and a
          low green landscape falling away behind the cups
      note: The setting is calm and open, with no cause for his stillness
          drawn into it. The numeral marks this as the fourth stage, where
          feeling has settled into a shape.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as settled feeling that has
    gone still, read sometimes as contentment and sometimes as a missing
    spark. The man is not unhappy, but he is turned inward, and the fourth cup
    held out to him is read as an offer that has gone unnoticed. Readers
    bring this card to moments of apathy, boredom, or reflection, where what
    is in front of a person has stopped registering. Whether the stillness is
    rest or a habit of not looking is left for the reader to decide.
invitationPrompt: What is already within reach today that you have stopped
    really looking at?
