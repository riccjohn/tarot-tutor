name: Justice
symbols:
    - element: A crowned figure seated square-on between two grey pillars,
          facing the viewer, in a red robe with a green cloak fastened by a
          square clasp
      note: The frontal, symmetrical pose is the card's argument. The figure
          sits still and level, the way a judge sits, and the pillars frame
          that stillness as a fixed structure rather than a passing moment.
    - element: A tall straight sword held upright in the right hand, blade
          rising past the top of the figure's head
      note: Conventionally read as the cutting edge of a decision, the
          part of justice that separates one thing from another. It is
          held vertical and steady, not pointed at anyone.
    - element: A pair of gold scales hanging from the left hand, each pan
          hung level on its own arm
      note: The scales are the older, plainer emblem of weighing. They are
          balanced in the picture, so the card shows the weighing settled
          rather than in progress.
    - element: A purple-red curtain hung between the pillars behind the
          figure, with a yellow band across the top carrying the numeral XI
      note: The curtain closes off the background completely, so there is
          no landscape to look at. Attention stays on the figure and the two
          instruments in her hands.
    - element: A small square-cut crown with a blue-green stone at its
          centre, and a red circular stone at the throat of the cloak
      note: The two matching jewels sit on the same vertical line as the
          sword, tying head and heart to the blade above them.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading takes this card as accountability and fair
    weighing, cause meeting consequence. After the turning of the Wheel that
    precedes it, the card asks what a person is answerable for, and what
    they would look at differently if they weighed it evenly. The sword and
    the scales pair two jobs, seeing a situation clearly and deciding what
    follows from it, and the card holds both at once.
invitationPrompt: What is one thing on your scales today that you have been
    weighing more lightly than it deserves?
