name: The World
symbols:
    - element: A large oval wreath of green leaves, tied with red ribbon at
          the top and bottom, enclosing the central figure
      note: The wreath is a closed ring, the card's clearest picture of a
          completed cycle. The red ties at both ends fasten it as a single
          finished form.
    - element: A figure in the middle of the wreath, mid-step on one foot,
          with a purple scarf wound around the body and blowing out on
          both sides
      note: The pose is a dance, not a stand. Completion here is shown as
          movement held inside a frame, and the scarf keeps that movement
          visible.
    - element: A slim white wand held in each hand, angled outward from
          the body
      note: The two matching wands are placed symmetrically, one on each
          side, in a gesture that reads as balance. Conventionally they are
          taken as the figure's command of two complementary forces.
    - element: Four heads in the corners, a human head at upper left, an
          eagle at upper right, a bull at lower left, and a lion at lower
          right, each set on a cloud
      note: The four corners form a frame around the wreath and are
          often read as four fixed points or elements, and the same four
          creatures turn up on the Wheel of Fortune.
    - element: A pale blue sky filling the space inside and around the
          wreath, with the numeral XXI at the top
      note: There is no ground or horizon drawn. The figure moves in open
          space, which supports reading the card as a place of arrival
          rather than of travel.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading takes this card as completion and
    integration, a cycle finished and a person at home in it. The wreath
    closes the picture and the figure inside it is at ease, moving freely
    within a form that has come together. Because the Fool at 0 stands
    outside the three rows, the sequence can be read as circling back to
    that opening, so the end of the run is also a place to begin again.
invitationPrompt: What is something you have finished that you have not
    yet let yourself notice as finished?
