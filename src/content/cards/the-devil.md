name: The Devil
symbols:
    - element: A large horned, bat-winged figure with a goat-like face, a
          raised open right hand, and an inverted pentagram between its horns
      note: The figure is drawn as a composite, part human, part animal, and
          it looms over everything else on the card. Conventionally it
          stands for appetite and compulsion given a face, not for an outside
          evil.
    - element: A torch held in the left hand, pointing downward, with its
          flame burning at the tip
      note: The flame is lit but aimed at the ground. It is usually read as
          desire that is burning with nowhere useful to go, and it echoes
          the flame on the tail of the figure beside it.
    - element: Two small nude figures, one on each side of a black block,
          each with small horns and a tail, one tail ending in grapes and the
          other in flame
      note: The tails match the figures to appetite and heat. Their
          horns suggest they are becoming like what holds them, which is
          the card's quiet point about attachment.
    - element: A chain running from a ring on the block up to a loop around
          each figure's neck
      note: The links hang loose and the loops are wide. Nothing in the
          picture shows the chains pulled taut or locked, so the card
          leaves open whether the figures could lift them off.
    - element: A solid black background, with a thin band of floor at the
          bottom and the numeral XV in the top centre
      note: There is no landscape and no way out drawn into the picture,
          which sets the card in a closed room of its own making.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading takes the Devil as attachment, the habits
    and bargains a person has taken on and might loosen. It concerns what
    holds someone, not an outside evil. The loose chains are the detail
    readers most often pause on, since they show a bond that can be lifted
    by the person wearing it. Opening the last row of the sequence, the card
    asks about the patterns a person meets as the final stage begins.
invitationPrompt: What is one habit or agreement you keep that you would
    want to look at again, if you knew you were free to?
