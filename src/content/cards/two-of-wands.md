---
name: Two of Wands
symbols:
    - element: A figure in a red cap and deep red cloak, seen in profile on a
          stone parapet, facing left and looking out over the land
      note: The figure is turned away from the viewer toward the horizon, so
          the card is about the view and the decision it prompts, more than
          about the person's face or feelings.
    - element: One wooden staff gripped in the figure's right hand, and a
          second staff standing upright at the right, fixed to the wall by an
          iron ring
      note: The pair is the Two made literal. One wand is held and mobile,
          the other is anchored in place, which suits a card about
          what is being carried and what is already fixed.
    - element: A small globe held in the figure's left hand
      note: Conventionally read as the wider world, or the scale of what could
          be attempted. It is held at chest height, within reach, not yet set
          in motion.
    - element: A wide view of a blue bay on the left and green cultivated
          land, a small red-roofed house, and mountains on the right
      note: Water on one side and settled land on the other gives the figure
          two directions to look, echoing the two wands and the card's number.
    - element: A stone wall below the figure, with a badge of crossed red
          roses and white lilies on its left face
      note: The emblem sits on the wall the figure stands behind. It is often
          read as the security already built, the place from which the
          looking out is being done.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a held balance between
    what is familiar and something larger. The figure has already achieved
    a comfortable, elevated position and is weighing whether to stay with
    it or push out toward what lies past the wall. Nothing has been chosen
    yet, and the card treats that weighing as a stage of its own. In Wands the
    choice is about ambition and direction, what is worth setting out for.
invitationPrompt: Which part of your view today feels settled, and which part
    keeps pulling your attention toward the horizon?
