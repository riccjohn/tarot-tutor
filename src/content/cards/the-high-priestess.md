name: The High Priestess
symbols:
    - element: A seated woman in pale blue robes with a white cross on her chest, facing the viewer directly
      note: She is still and centred, and she looks straight out of the card. Conventionally read as someone who waits and holds knowledge rather than acting on it, in contrast to the raised arm of the Magician before her.
    - element: Two tall pillars, one black and marked B, one white and marked J, standing on either side of her
      note: The pillars are opposites set at equal height with her between them. Often read as a pair of polarities, such as dark and light or yes and no, that she sits between without choosing one.
    - element: A scroll partly tucked beneath her robe, with the letters TORA visible on it
      note: Only part of the text can be read, and the rest is covered. Often read as knowledge that is held but not fully disclosed.
    - element: A crown of a full disc between two crescent horns, and a crescent moon lying at her feet
      note: Both are lunar shapes, one above her head and one below her feet. Conventionally tied to intuition, cycles, and knowing that comes without being explained.
    - element: A curtain patterned with pomegranates and palm fronds, hanging in the gap between the pillars behind her
      note: The curtain closes off whatever lies beyond it, with only thin strips of blue showing at its edges. Read as a threshold she guards, with the fruit often taken as fertility or something held within.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading sets this card against the activity of the one before it. The Magician does, and she waits and knows. She is often read as intuition, privacy, and the value of what is not yet said. The pillars and the veil suggest a threshold with something behind it that she keeps rather than explains. On this reading the card suggests that some understanding arrives by listening more than by acting.
