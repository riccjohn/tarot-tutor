name: King of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A crowned figure seated upright and facing the viewer directly,
          in a pale blue robe with a grey-purple cloak lined in red and a red
          hood beneath a gold crown
      note: The frontal, symmetrical pose is the King's rank. Where the
          Queen sits in profile, he sits square to the viewer, addressing
          whoever is looking.
    - element: A long straight sword held upright in one hand at his side,
          tilted slightly to the left, with the point above the crown
      note: The blade is raised and steady, not pointed at anyone. It reads
          as authority held ready, the suit's clear judgment used to rule
          rather than to fight.
    - element: The other hand resting open on his lap, next to the heavy folds
          of his robe
      note: The free hand is still and relaxed. Control is displayed by what
          is not being done, with no gesture of impatience.
    - element: A tall black throne-back behind him carved with butterfly
          shapes and small face-like forms in pale relief
      note: The butterflies are the card's one delicate detail, set behind
          the sternest figure of the suit. Often read as thought given
          shape and lightness, or as change kept within order.
    - element: A few birds high in the pale blue sky at the right, grey
          clouds low on either side, and dark trees on a green ridge with an
          orange path in front
      note: A clear, open sky and a wide landscape, with the King at its
          centre. The setting is calm, so the card's weight rests entirely
          on the figure and the sword.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats the King of Swords as mastery of
    thought, with authority resting on clear judgment and honest speech. He is
    the stage at which someone stops learning the suit and begins deciding
    with it, taking responsibility for what follows from a considered call.
    As a person, he is a mentor, a judge, or anyone with real command of a
    field who says what is true and holds to it. The rank carries its own
    hazard as well, because full control can harden into rigidity, and the
    strong version of the card keeps the Page's curiosity alive inside the
    authority.
invitationPrompt: Where are you being asked to make a call today, and what
    would it look like to make it fairly and still stay open to hearing
    otherwise?
