name: Ten of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A figure lying face-down and motionless on bare ground, one
          hand extended with the fingers curled, a red cloth spread out
          beside them
      note: The image is an overwhelming one, and the card treats it as a
          picture of exhaustion and defeat at the end of a long contest of
          thought, not of anything that happens to the reader's body. The
          figure has stopped, and the ground is flat and quiet.
    - element: Ten swords standing upright in a loose row along the length of
          the figure's back, their blades vertical and their hilts pointing
          to the sky
      note: The full count of the suit, every one used, so the card is the
          Ten as the complete and final weight of the suit's argument. There
          are no swords left to draw.
    - element: A band of pale yellow light stretching across the horizon
          beneath a black, starry sky
      note: The dark sky covers most of the card, and the yellow band is the
          first light at its edge. Conventionally read as the point where the
          worst has passed and the day begins to come back.
    - element: A flat blue-grey stretch of still water and a line of distant
          low hills beyond the figure
      note: The water is calm and reaches to the horizon. It gives the scene
          an open, settled far side that the foreground does not have.
    - element: The numeral X in white at the top of the black sky
      note: The number of completion. The cycle that began with a single
          hand and one sword in the Ace has run its full length.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as the exhausted end of a
    hard stretch, where the worst has passed and the ground is clear again.
    The Ten completes the suit, so a way of thinking, arguing, or defending
    that has been carried as far as it goes has reached its limit. The band
    of light along the horizon is often read as the reason the card is
    less bleak than it first looks, since the sequence is over, and something
    new can start from level ground. This is a card of ending and release
    of a pattern of thought or a conflict, not a forecast about anyone's
    body or life.
invitationPrompt: What is one way of fighting or worrying that you would be
    glad to stop, and what might you do with the time it frees up?
