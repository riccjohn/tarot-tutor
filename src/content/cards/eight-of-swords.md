name: Eight of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A figure in a long red dress, standing upright with a grey band
          across the eyes and grey cloth wound loosely around the arms and
          torso
      note: The wrappings cross the body in a few loose loops, and the feet
          are free. Conventionally read as a constraint that looks tighter
          than it is, or that is partly self-placed.
    - element: Eight swords planted upright in the ground around the figure,
          four to the left and four to the right, with wide gaps between
          them
      note: The blades stand well apart, and none of them touches the figure.
          Nothing in front of the figure is fenced, so the enclosure is a
          matter of how it is seen, not a sealed wall.
    - element: Shallow pools of pale blue water spread across the muddy
          ground at the figure's feet
      note: The ground is wet and uneven but walkable. It sets the stage for
          a slow first step and leaves no obvious hazard beyond the mud.
    - element: A rocky grey cliff on the right with a small castle on its
          summit, red-roofed and distant
      note: A place of shelter or authority in the background, well outside
          the ring of swords. It reads as somewhere to head, or as a
          reminder that the wider world is still there.
    - element: A blank grey sky, with the numeral VIII across the top
      note: The empty sky adds no drama, so the card's tension sits entirely
          in the blindfold, the wrapping, and the ring of blades.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as the feeling of being
    hemmed in, where the limits turn out to be more permeable than they
    appear. The Eight is a stage of committed movement, and in Swords
    attention has narrowed until the blindfold and the loose wrapping seem
    like the whole situation. Readers note that the swords do not close
    off the front and that the figure's feet are unbound. On that reading
    the card describes a stuck feeling more than a fixed fact, and it puts
    the next step at the reader's own pace. Whether the way out is a
    change of view or a change of place is the reader's to decide.
invitationPrompt: Which of the limits you feel today would you test first if
    you could take one small step to check whether it holds?
