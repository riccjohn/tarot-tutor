name: Seven of Swords
symbols:
    - element: A figure in a red cap, olive tunic, and blue leggings, striding
          on the toes of red boots, glancing back over their shoulder with a
          faint smile
      note: The posture is light-footed and quiet. Conventionally read as
          moving without being noticed, and the sideways look shows that the
          figure is watching the camp they are leaving, not the ground ahead.
    - element: Five swords gathered in the figure's arms, their blades fanned
          across the chest and pointing forward and up
      note: The blades are carried loosely and bunched together, in a grip
          that looks only just secure. The figure has taken as many as one
          pair of arms can hold.
    - element: Two swords left standing upright in the ground at the right,
          hilts pointing skyward
      note: Two of the seven are left behind. The card does not say why, so
          they read as either a choice, a limit of what could be carried, or
          a plan that stopped short.
    - element: A tented camp at the right edge, with striped and patterned
          canvas, a small flag, and a group of figures gathered near a fire at
          the far left
      note: The camp is close and occupied, and the figure walks with their
          back to it. The scene sets the card in the middle of an
          arrangement that other people are still part of.
    - element: A saturated yellow sky filling the top of the card, with the
          numeral VII above the figure's head
      note: A flat, bright backdrop with no shadow or cloud, so nothing on
          the card hides the figure from view except the figure's own
          quiet.
status: review
commonReading: One common reading treats this card as a plan that depends on
    strategy or on working alone. Readers split on the tone. Some see
    cleverness, a well-chosen approach that spares everyone a direct
    confrontation. Others see avoidance, a way of dodging the plain
    conversation that would settle things. Either reading turns on the same
    detail, which is that the figure is carrying the swords quietly instead
    of announcing what they are doing. The Seven is a test of commitment, and
    here it is a test of whether the approach being taken is one the person
    can stand behind if it were seen.
invitationPrompt: Is there something you are handling quietly right now that
    you would handle differently if the people involved could see it?
