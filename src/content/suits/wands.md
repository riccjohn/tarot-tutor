suit: wands
name: Wands
domain: Will, ambition, creative drive, and the energy to begin and keep going.
conventionalElement: Fire
contested: Wands' elemental attribution is genuinely disputed between
    traditions. The scheme most familiar from the 1909 deck's usual
    commentary pairs Wands with Fire and Swords with Air; some earlier
    continental systems reverse that pairing, giving Wands to Air and Swords
    to Fire. This site follows the Fire-for-Wands scheme because it is the
    one the surrounding structural material assumes, not because the
    question is settled.
status: review
---

Wands is the suit of will in motion: the spark of an idea, the decision to act
on it, the stretch of effort that follows, and the friction of meeting other
people's plans along the way. Where Swords weighs reasons and Cups follows
feeling, Wands follows momentum. Every card in the suit shows a wooden staff,
often still carrying leaves, which suits a subject that is about growth and
drive as much as about force: a wand can be a walking staff, a banner pole, a
barrier, or a torch, depending on what is being carried and where.

The suit's fourteen cards (ten pips plus four court cards) trace a project or
a passion from its first unformed impulse through confidence, competition, and
the long middle where persistence is the whole task, and on to the point where
a person has to decide what is worth carrying any further. It is the suit most
concerned with vocation, creativity, and ambition, and it is often the most
restless: Wands cards tend to be mid-gesture, leaning forward, waiting on the
next move.

Traditionally paired with the element Fire: energy, enthusiasm, and the will
that turns wanting into doing. That pairing is not universal. The disagreement
with Swords is the standing one in tarot, and which suit takes Fire and which
takes Air depends on the tradition you learned from. See the note on contested
attribution above. Either way the suit holds together as a way of reading:
Wands cards are asking what you are moving toward and what is driving you,
more than they are asking what is true or what is felt.
