Pentacles is the suit of the material world: work, money, home, craft, health
as a matter of daily habit, and the slow business of turning effort into
something that lasts. Where Wands starts things and Cups feels them, Pentacles
tends them. Every card in the suit shows a coin marked with a five-pointed
star, and a coin is a good emblem for the suit's subject: something valued,
something that can be earned, saved, spent, or handed on, and something you
can put in your hand and count.

The suit's fourteen cards (ten pips plus four court cards) follow the work of
building a life or a livelihood, from the first opportunity or offer, through
careful saving, apprenticeship, and generosity, into the patience of waiting on
a harvest and the security of something established. It is the suit most
concerned with practical questions, and readers sometimes pass over it for
that reason. Its cards are often the most direct about ordinary concerns: what
a person is investing in, what they are getting back, and whether the effort
matches the reward.

Traditionally paired with the element Earth: stability, patience, and the
physical world as it actually is. Of the four suits this attribution is among
the least disputed. The suit itself goes by several names. Coins and Disks are
common alternatives, and the name a reader knows usually reflects the deck they
learned on, not a difference in meaning. Pentacles cards are asking what is
solid and what is being built, more than they are asking what is true, wanted,
or felt.
