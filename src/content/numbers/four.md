Four is the first number that builds something stable: four corners, four
legs, four walls. After the Three's complication, the Four is a pause in which
the situation gets a shape it can stand on, and the cards tend to show
arrangements that are enclosed, held, or set in place.

One common reading treats the Four as stability that can be taken two ways
within the same card: as a foundation worth having, or as a structure that has
started to feel closed in. Which of those a reading emphasizes is up to the
reader and the cards around it. The Four looks different in each suit. In
Wands it is a completed piece of work and the chance to celebrate it; in Cups
it is settled feeling that has gone a little still, read sometimes as
contentment and sometimes as a missing spark; in Swords it is deliberate rest,
a stretch of quiet in which the mind is allowed to stop; in Pentacles it is
holding tightly to what has been built. The throughline holds across every
suit: something has been made steady, and the question is what that
steadiness is for.
