Seven has long been treated as a number of assessment. It is the point where
six steps of effort have accumulated and something is asked of what they
produced. The cards typically show a single figure at a decision point or
holding a position against pressure, with the next move left open.

One common reading treats the Seven as a test of commitment: does the reader
keep going, stand firm, reconsider, or change course? It is less about the
outcome than about the decision itself. The test takes a different form in
each suit. In Wands it is defending a position against challengers; in Cups it
is a crowd of possibilities and the difficulty of telling which are real; in
Swords it is a plan that depends on strategy or on working alone, read
sometimes as cleverness and sometimes as avoiding a direct approach; in
Pentacles it is patience, looking over the work and asking whether it is worth
continuing. The throughline is the same throughout: this is the moment to
evaluate, before committing to the next stage.
