Every suit begins with an Ace, and every Ace is drawn the same way: a single
hand reaching out of a cloud, holding one emblem. There is no scene around it,
no second figure, nothing yet to compare it to. Structurally the Ace is the
smallest unit a suit can be, which is why it is the one pip card that shows
the suit's symbol on its own, unattached to any situation.

One common reading treats the Ace as pure potential: the moment before an
idea, a feeling, a job, or a piece of work has been given any direction. It is
an offer more than an event, and what it becomes is decided by the numbers
that follow. That reading holds across all four suits, and it lands
differently in each. In Wands the offer is a spark of drive, a first push of
energy; in Cups it is a feeling arriving before there are words for it; in
Swords it is a single clear thought, sharp before it has been tested against
anything; in Pentacles it is something concrete and available, like an
opportunity, a resource, or a first bit of groundwork. The number's
throughline stays the same in each: this is a beginning that has not
committed to anything yet, and what to do with it is open.
