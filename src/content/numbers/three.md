If Aces are pure potential and Twos are the first pairing — a choice, a
balance, a partnership just forming — Threes are where that pairing produces
something you can see. An idea becomes a plan in motion, a feeling becomes a
gathering, a skill becomes shared work. Threes across the deck tend to mark
the first real result: the point where something private steps out into the
world and other people start to have a hand in it.

That result isn't uniformly happy. In Wands, Cups and Pentacles a Three is
usually expansion, celebration or collaboration. In Swords, the suit most
concerned with sharp truths, the first thing to come into the open is often
painful — which is why the Three of Swords is read as one of the deck's more
emotionally direct cards. The number's throughline stays the same in every
suit: what started as a seed now has visible consequences, for better or
worse.
