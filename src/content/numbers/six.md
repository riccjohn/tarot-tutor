Six follows the Five's strain with relief. The imagery settles into an
arrangement: figures traveling together, a gift passing between two people, a
shared task moving forward. Where Five is a break, Six is the first step back
toward order, and it is often the first number in which someone in the scene
is helping someone else.

One common reading treats the Six as a passage through difficulty toward
something steadier, with give and take built into it. That reading fits each
suit in its own way. In Wands it is a recognized win, effort acknowledged in
public; in Cups it is generosity or memory, feeling passed from one person to
another; in Swords it is a quiet crossing away from trouble, carrying the
lessons along with the load; in Pentacles it is the balance of giving and
receiving, and how fairly it is shared. The throughline holds across the
suits: the pressure of the Five has eased, and what moves between people is
the mark of the number.
