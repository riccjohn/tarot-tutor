Eight is a number of momentum. After the Seven's assessment, the figures on
the Eights are in motion or absorbed in repeated work, and the compositions
often show eight of the same object lined up in a row or sequence. The number
carries a feeling of pace.

One common reading treats the Eight as a stage of committed movement, where
attention has narrowed to the task in front of the reader. It can be read as
momentum or as constraint, depending on the suit and the reader. In Wands it
is speed, several things arriving at once; in Cups it is a deliberate walking
away from something that no longer satisfies; in Swords it is a sense of being
hemmed in, where the limits are often more permeable than they appear; in
Pentacles it is craft, skill built through repetition. The throughline holds
across the suits: a decision has been made and the reader is living inside it,
whether that feels like flow or like a narrow corridor.
