The four court ranks are one ladder climbed in every suit, and the Page is its first rung. Where the pip cards run Ace through Ten, the court cards step outside that count. They are figures rather than situations, and each one stands in the suit's landscape holding the suit's emblem.

One common reading treats the court cards as people, roles, or stages of maturity in a suit's energy, and the Page is the stage of beginning. A Page is usually shown young, standing still or moving lightly, looking at the emblem in hand as though it were new. That is the whole position: the suit's energy has just arrived and is being examined, not yet used.

The Page reads as a message, a first attempt, or an early curiosity. As a person, the Page is a student, a newcomer, or someone approaching a subject with fewer assumptions than the people around them. As a role, it is the one who asks the plain question. As a stage, it is the point before mastery, where enthusiasm runs ahead of skill and the gap is allowed to be there.

Traditions name this rank differently. Some decks call it the Knave or the Princess, and the ladder is ordered differently in some of those systems. This site uses Page, Knight, Queen, King, and treats the Page as the youngest and least settled of the four.

The same Page reads differently across suits. A Page of Swords is learning to think and speak precisely; a Page of Cups is learning to feel and say so. What stays constant is the posture: attention first, action later.
