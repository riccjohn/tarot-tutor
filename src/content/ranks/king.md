The King is the fourth and final rung of the court ladder, and like the Queen he is shown seated, usually upright and facing forward. He sits on a throne with the suit's emblem in hand, and the suit's landscape stretches out behind him. This rank completes the climb that began with the Page.

One common reading treats the court cards as people, roles, or stages of maturity in a suit's energy, and the King is the stage of mastery. The suit's energy is fully known, and the question is now what to do with authority over it. Where the Queen understands from within, the King acts outward: he decides, directs, and takes responsibility for what follows.

The King reads as leadership, expertise, and final say. As a person, he is a mentor, a manager, or anyone with real command of a field. As a stage, it is the point where someone stops learning the suit and begins shaping things with it. The rank has its own hazard, since full control can harden into rigidity, and a King who no longer listens has stopped being a master and become an obstacle. The strong version keeps the Page's curiosity alive inside the authority.

Traditions disagree about the order of the court. Some place the King as the most active figure and the Knight as the more restrained, and others reorder the four altogether. This site follows the order of Page, Knight, Queen, King, and names that as one common arrangement rather than the only one.

The King of Swords rules by clear judgment and honest speech, and the King of Cups by mature, steady feeling. The suit says what he commands, and the rank says that he commands it.
