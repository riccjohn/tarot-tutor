The Knight is the second rung of the court ladder, and it is the rank most often shown on horseback. Where the Page stands and looks, the Knight is going somewhere. The horse may be charging, walking, or holding still under a rider who is plainly ready to move, but the rank is defined by direction.

One common reading treats the court cards as people, roles, or stages of maturity in a suit's energy, and the Knight is the stage of pursuit. The suit's energy is no longer being examined; it is being committed to. A Knight has a quest, and the quest is taken seriously, sometimes more seriously than the situation calls for.

The Knight reads as momentum: a project pushed forward, a person acting on conviction, a phase where doing matters more than deliberating. The strength of the rank is follow-through. The cost is single-mindedness. Enthusiasm comes with tunnel vision, and the same drive that gets things moving can overshoot what the moment needed.

Traditions disagree about where the Knight sits. In some systems it is the young adult figure, and in others it is a Prince who stands closer to the King. This site puts the Knight between Page and Queen, as the stage where the suit's energy is at its most active and least tempered.

The Knight of Swords rides hard on an idea; the Knight of Cups follows a feeling with the same intensity. The suit changes what is pursued. The intensity of the pursuit belongs to the Knight.
